Andean Transit Migration Data Hub

The questions you would ask us

Count the crossing, never the person. Short answers, in plain words.

1What is the Hub, in one sentence?

A free data system, run by young people, that tells each town on Bolivia's migration road how many people are coming, months ahead, and never knows who they are.

2How do you make the forecast, and how accurate is it?

Today it is the simplest method there is: it assumes the next months look like the recent ones, and it shows a wide margin of error. We chose that on purpose. We have seven months of data, and an honest accuracy test needs twenty-four. When we have them, we will publish the accuracy town by town, including where it fails.

3With small numbers, how do you stop someone being identified?

Three locks. We never collect a name or an ID. Any group smaller than ten is hidden automatically, and the figures are rounded. And no code follows a person from one town to the next.

4Who owns the data? What stops a government from asking for more?

The records stay on our offline laptop. A government gets the screen with totals, never the records. Even if it asks, there is nothing to hand over: we do not have names.

5What happens after the prize? Who maintains it if nobody is paid?

It was built to cost almost nothing: free software, no server, no licence. The Youth Council exists by law and renews its volunteers every year. And once a town is trained, its own staff runs it.

6Why should a town trust student volunteers?

Because they do not have to trust us. The code is open, the privacy rule runs by itself, and the Council that backs us was created by law. Bolivia's Director General of Migration already said it: the numbers are theirs.

7412 of 900 is 46 percent. Why does your screen say 47?

They are two different figures. Forty-six percent is the share of migrants who agreed to be counted. Forty-seven percent, on the platform, is the share of arrivals in Santa Cruz who found paid work within sixty days.

8Is the government using it already?

Not yet, and we say so. Officials in El Alto have been trained, and the migration authority has met with us three times. The prize is what takes it to the border towns.

9What does the first prize buy?

EUR 3,000 takes the Hub to the three towns where people leave Bolivia: Pisiga, Villazón and Yacuiba, at EUR 1,000 each. EUR 1,500 pays bus tickets and meals for fifteen counting campaigns. EUR 900 pays a two-day workshop for local officials in each town. EUR 600 prints permission cards and rights guides for migrants, in Spanish and in Indigenous languages. Nothing goes to salaries.
